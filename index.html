Ninety days when the city moves outdoors

Dubai's season runs October to December. This is what is actually on, what it costs, and which version of the city each visitor gets.

Why October to December

The heat breaks, the calendar fills, and the city moves outdoors. Everything here sits inside that window.

Climate

Daytime settles into the mid-20s to low-30s. Terraces, beaches and desert camps become comfortable again.

Programme

The heaviest run of festivals, concerts, sport and trade shows in the year, most of it outdoors.

Business

GITEX GLOBAL and MEBAA put a large share of the world's tech and aviation buyers here in one week.

Finish

The Shopping Festival and the 31 December fireworks close the quarter with the city's two biggest draws.

The city before the towers

Dubai's cultural quarter sits on the creek, costs almost nothing, and is where the city's story actually starts.

  • Etihad MuseumBuilt where the UAE was founded. Documents and exhibits covering 1968 to 1974.
  • Al Fahidi Historic DistrictThe best-preserved quarter in the city: wind towers, museums, courtyard cafes.
  • Gold and Spice Souks, DeiraThe working markets. Haggling is expected, and the abra connects both banks.
  • Jumeirah MosqueOne of the few mosques in the UAE open to non-Muslim visitors, by guided tour.

Free, and genuinely good

  • Public ground, DowntownFree to watch, but arrive several hours early. Roads around the tower close.
